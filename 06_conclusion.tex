\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Minimum instability witnesses identify the smallest permitted source
intervention that crosses an aggregate threshold. COUNT and nonnegative
SUM yield weighted coverage. Extrema add a survivor obligation, leading
to a complete feasibility test and a combined $\min\{r,1+\ln m\}$
approximation. AVG yields a signed linear threshold, with exact algorithms
for singleton supports and a coverage guarantee under sign separation.
The single-deletable-relation results expose the importance of source
reuse: self-join-free queries are tractable, while two occurrences of the
deletable relation already admit fixed-query hardness.

Execution depends on the representation as well as the optimization rule.
Incremental supports, certified lazy SQL, and multilinear derivatives
compute the same coverage marginals through different operations. The
TPC-H-derived pilot confirms this agreement and shows both an incremental
greedy speed advantage and an AVG case where ILP is faster. These findings
motivate workload-specific execution choices rather than a uniform ranking
of exact and heuristic methods.

A fuller query classification, compact survivor selection, and broader
experiments are natural next steps. Duplicate elimination, null values,
grouped outputs, signed SUM, repair constraints, and non-unit costs also
raise new modeling and algorithmic questions. The present framework
provides explicit threshold semantics and correctness conditions against
which such extensions can be compared.
